## Type Selection Guide

Choose the right type based on the request:
- **Date/time/activity people participate in** → Community Event

- **Community question, complaint, or feedback you're responding to** → Feedback Response

### TYPE: RESPONSE TO COMMUNITY FEEDBACK

Write a community-facing response to feedback, a complaint, or a common concern.

**Principles:**
- Acknowledge the specific feedback first — don't jump to defending
- Be honest about what you know, what you don't know, and what you can't share yet
- If it's a valid concern: say so
- If it's being worked on: say what you can about the timeline
- If you can't do it: be direct but kind about why
- Never dismiss or get defensive
- Invite continued dialogue

[Acknowledge the feedback specifically — quote or paraphrase what people are saying]

[Your honest take — do you agree? Is it on the roadmap? What context might they not know?]

[What you're doing about it, or why it is the way it is]

[Invitation: ask a follow-up question or invite them to share more]

— [Name or "The INWIGO Team"]

### TYPE: REDDIT POST

Write a post for r/VRGaming, r/gaming, or a specific subreddit.

**Reddit rewards**: transparency, real news, genuine discussion, behind-the-scenes honesty, milestones with context.
**Reddit punishes**: obvious marketing, low-effort promotions, posting without engaging in comments.

Always be upfront that you're the developer in the first line.

**Structure:**

[Title — follow subreddit conventions. Often: `[INWIGO] [What you're sharing]` or a genuine question]

---

Hey r/[subreddit]! I'm [name/role] on INWIGO — [brief genuine opener].

[Main content — news, screenshot, clip, milestone, or genuine question. Lead with the interesting thing.]

[Behind-the-scenes context — the honest story behind this. What was hard? What surprised you? This is what Reddit rewards.]

[Engage — ask a specific question to invite discussion. "Has anyone else found that [specific thing] in VR?"]

[Links clearly labeled — not buried:]

### Voice Reminders for Community

- These are **your people** — treat them like it, not like a mailing list
- Personality > polish in community spaces
- When in doubt, be more human and less brand
- Emojis: yes, tastefully — 2–5 per post, not per sentence
- Humor: encouraged, but never at a community member's expense
