## Instructions by Type

Identify the recipient type from the request and follow the appropriate approach.

---

### TYPE: PRESS / GAMING JOURNALIST

**Philosophy**: Journalists get dozens of pitches a day. Yours needs to answer "why should I care about THIS game for MY readers?" in the first two sentences.

**What to include:**
- Subject line that's specific, intriguing, 5-8 words
- Opening that references their specific beat or recent coverage (make it clear you've read their work)
- The news hook — what's the actual story (launch, demo, milestone, angle)?
- Why it's interesting for their specific audience
- Clear ask: review copy / preview access / embargo details
- What you're providing: key, press kit, interview availability
- Simple next step
- Under 200 words total

**Template:**
```
SUBJECT: [Specific, intriguing — e.g., "VR's answer to Lethal Company launches [date]"]

Hi [Name],

[One specific reference to their work — a recent article, their coverage area, or their publication's focus. Make it real, not generic.]

[The hook — what is INWIGO, why is it newsworthy NOW, and why does it fit their readers? 2-3 sentences max.]

[The ask — clear and direct. Review copy available? Preview build? Interview with developer?]

[What you're providing: key via [platform], press kit at [URL], happy to answer questions.]

[Simple close — one question or one easy next step]

[Name]
INWIGO
[Email]
[Press kit URL]
```

### TYPE: VR / HORROR CONTENT CREATOR / STREAMER

**Philosophy**: Creators need content that will perform well with *their* audience. Your pitch needs to answer: "will my viewers watch this?" INWIGO is co-op chaos in VR — that's extremely content-creator-friendly. Lead with that.

**What to include:**
- Subject line that's about them, not you
- Reference to their specific content style (show you've actually watched their stuff)
- Why INWIGO is perfect for their audience (clippable moments, co-op chaos, VR jump scares)
- The offer: early access key, press kit
- Optional: offer to play a session with them — this creates content AND relationship
- The soft ask: just try it / stream it / make a video if you like it
- Under 150 words

**Template:**
```
SUBJECT: [Make it about their content / audience — e.g., "VR co-op chaos for your horror fans"]

Hi [Name],

[Specific reference to their recent content or their audience vibe — e.g., "Your Phasmophobia reactions are exactly the energy INWIGO was built for."]

[What INWIGO is in one sentence — the comp-based pitch: "It's Lethal Company meets Don't Starve, but you're actually in VR."]

[Why it's content-creator-gold: 1-2 sentences on clippable moments, the co-op chaos, the jump scares, the emergent gameplay]

[The offer: "I'd love to send you an early access key." + Optional: "Happy to jump in a session with you if you want an extra player."]

[Easy close — no pressure, no obligation]

[Name]
INWIGO Team
[Your email]
```

### TYPE: GAMING MEDIA / PUBLICATION PARTNERSHIP

More formal than a creator pitch. Include:
- Introduction to the game and developer
- Specific partnership proposal (coverage deal, exclusive, sponsored coverage — be clear)
- Mutual value proposition
- What you're offering
- What you're requesting
- Next steps / call to schedule

---

### TYPE: INFLUENCER FOLLOW-UP (after no response)

Keep it warm and brief — 3-4 sentences max. Reference the original pitch, add one new piece of information (milestone, trailer, upcoming demo), re-extend the offer. Don't grovel or over-explain.

---

### Email Quality Standards

Every outreach email should pass this check:
- [ ] Subject line is specific and non-generic (not "VR Game Pitch")
- [ ] First line proves you know who you're writing to
- [ ] The hook is clear in the first 2 sentences
- [ ] The ask is explicit (don't make them guess what you want)
- [ ] Reads like a person wrote it, not a PR department
- [ ] Under the word limit for that type
- [ ] Includes all necessary links / assets

---

### Tone Reminder

**Do**: Genuine enthusiasm, specific knowledge of their work, clear value exchange
**Don't**: Generic templates, overselling, jargon ("synergy", "partnership opportunity"), multiple asks in one email
